# Limitations

Each entry: **Limitation / Evidence / Impact / Why it remains / Next validation step.**
Quantities are in [evaluation/RESULTS.md](evaluation/RESULTS.md) (generated);
none are restated here.

### L1. Subjects are demonstration and teaching code
- **Evidence:** CardDemo (AWS modernization demo), OMP COBOL course labs, GenApp installation JCL. No production JCL was available.
- **Impact:** Job sizes, construct mix (IDCAMS-heavy, few COND guards, no cross-job scheduler definitions) and defect patterns may not represent production batch systems. Results do not generalise beyond these corpora.
- **Why it remains:** No production corpus with a permissive license was available.
- **Next step:** Evaluate on production or industrial JCL under an NDA, or on more public corpora (e.g. Zowe/IBM samples), reporting per-subject results.

### L2. The primary subject dominates
- **Evidence:** CardDemo provides most evaluable mutants. The OMP course contributes few, mostly cosmetic negatives. GenApp is installation JCL whose dependencies sit largely in DB2 DDL that the oracle treats as opaque.
- **Impact:** Pooled numbers mostly reflect CardDemo, so external validity is weak.
- **Why it remains:** The secondary subjects contain few multi-step jobs with dataset dependencies.
- **Next step:** Add subjects with richer batch streams. Report per-subject results only.

### L3. The oracle is a model, not an execution
- **Evidence:** `jcl_oracle` simulates JCL semantics from the IBM reference. It has not been run against z/OS. It was written by the same remediation effort and has not been reviewed by a mainframe practitioner. One shared blind spot (same-named steps) was found and fixed during evaluation.
- **Impact:** Labels may be wrong where the model departs from real behaviour (opaque programs, SMS assumptions, RC domain, single-fault scenarios). Other blind spots shared with RELIC may remain.
- **Why it remains:** No z/OS access. No independent annotator.
- **Next step:** (a) a mainframe engineer audits a stratified sample of oracle labels blind to RELIC's predictions; (b) execute a sample of original/mutant pairs on z/OS (e.g. IBM Z Development and Test Environment) and compare.

### L4. Abend semantics make every reorder observable
- **Evidence:** Under the primary scenario set, swapping two steps always changes which steps run when the first abends. The `no_abend` sensitivity analysis gives different precision.
- **Impact:** RELIC's precision on reorderings depends on whether failure behaviour is considered observable. Both views are reported; neither is "the" answer.
- **Why it remains:** It is a definitional choice. The primary definition was fixed before results were computed.
- **Next step:** Ask practitioners which observables matter for migration acceptance, and weight results accordingly.

### L5. Single-file unit of analysis
- **Evidence:** RELIC, the baselines and the oracle all analyse one JCL file. Renames whose consequence lies in other jobs are OUT_OF_SCOPE.
- **Impact:** Cross-job dependencies, which are common in CardDemo (one job creates a VSAM file another job reads), are not evaluated.
- **Why it remains:** Cross-job order comes from a scheduler, and CardDemo ships no scheduler definitions.
- **Next step:** Model job networks from scheduler definitions where available, and evaluate RELIC's cross-artifact RESOURCE contracts.

### L6. Synthetic faults, unverified taxonomy
- **Evidence:** Mutants are generated by operators. The earlier "empirical grounding" sources could not be verified ([failure_taxonomy.md](failure_taxonomy.md)).
- **Impact:** Recall on synthetic mutants may not equal recall on real migration defects.
- **Why it remains:** No corpus of real JCL migration defects is publicly available.
- **Next step:** Collect real migration defects (issue trackers, practitioner interviews) and compare them with the operators.

### L7. Benchmark positives are easy to flag
- **Evidence:** A detector that flags every non-comment change reaches high recall and F1, because most fault mutants change behaviour.
- **Impact:** F1 alone overstates how much RELIC adds. Its value is in specificity and in explaining *which* contract broke.
- **Why it remains:** It is a property of mutation benchmarks with few equivalent mutants.
- **Next step:** Add realistic harmless changes (renaming steps consistently, reformatting, moving independent steps in jobs without abend sensitivity) and report specificity at fixed recall.

### L8. RELIC v1.1 is post hoc
- **Evidence:** The v1.1 fixes (continuation rule, COND semantics, parameter-aware diff) were made after the audit and after first benchmark runs.
- **Impact:** v1.1 results are optimistic. Only v1.0.0 is a blind result.
- **Why it remains:** Fixing real defects was necessary; re-blinding is impossible on the same benchmark.
- **Next step:** Freeze v1.1 and evaluate it on new subjects not used during the fixes.

### L9. Known RELIC limitations visible in the results
- **Evidence:** Per-operator and contract-recovery tables. Procedures are not expanded. Same-named steps collapse into one entity. IF/THEN/ELSE is not parsed. RELIC v1.1 reports a changed COND guard only if it became *weaker*; a stricter guard also changes behaviour but is not reported. The benchmark's threshold operator always doubles the code, which weakens the common `LT` form, so this gap is not measured. IDCAMS-created datasets and program-library dependencies are not RESOURCE contracts. Producer/consumer pairs are formed regardless of order.
- **Impact:** Misses and spurious RESOURCE contracts. Low RESOURCE recovery recall.
- **Why it remains:** Out of the remediation's scope (no feature work tuned to the benchmark).
- **Next step:** Implement these as separately evaluated improvements on held-out subjects.

### L10. LLM comparison is narrow
- **Evidence:** `results/final/raw/llm_status.json` and the LLM table in `docs/evaluation/RESULTS.md`. One model (Groq `openai/gpt-oss-120b`), one prompt, temperature 0, one sample per mutant. Coverage depends on the provider quota of the API key used; unanswered mutants are excluded and all detectors are compared on the common answered population.
- **Impact:** The result says how this model with this prompt compares with RELIC on these mutants. It does not generalise to LLM-based review in general. The behaviour definition in the prompt makes the two harmless operators easy for the model.
- **Why it remains:** API access was limited to one free-tier provider key during the remediation.
- **Next step:** Several models, several samples, prompts without the MSGCLASS/comment hint, and full coverage. The cached responses make each run re-scorable offline.

### L11. Dynamic evidence is not validated
- **Evidence:** 38 of 40 legacy traces are step-order simulations derived from RELIC's own parser. Two real GnuCOBOL runs do not map to CardDemo contracts.
- **Impact:** The reconciliation component (confidence, validation states) has no empirical support. It plays no part in the reported detection results (static BCGs only).
- **Why it remains:** No z/OS or full GnuCOBOL+JCL execution environment.
- **Next step:** Collect real job logs (SMF/JES output) for CardDemo runs and reconcile against them.

### L12. Statistical power
- **Evidence:** A few hundred evaluable mutants clustered in tens of files. Intervals use a file-level bootstrap; Wilson intervals ignore clustering.
- **Impact:** Intervals are wide for secondary subjects and per-operator cells. Many per-operator cells are too small to interpret.
- **Why it remains:** Limited by corpus size.
- **Next step:** More subjects (L1).

### L13. No third-party external validation
- **Evidence:** The three subjects were chosen, prepared and evaluated by the RELIC authors, with an oracle written by the same team.
- **Impact:** This is a multi-subject evaluation, not an external validation. Independent replication has not happened.
- **Why it remains:** Requires other researchers or practitioners.
- **Next step:** Release the benchmark and invite replication on production or other public JCL.

### L14. Contract coverage
- **Evidence:** `ContractType` declares DATA, STATE and ERROR, but no extractor produces them and none is evaluated.
- **Impact:** Field-level data compatibility, state initialisation and error-propagation obligations are not checked at all.
- **Why it remains:** Not implemented. Adding them only to make the evaluation look broader would not be meaningful.
- **Next step:** Implement and evaluate them separately, with their own oracle observables.
